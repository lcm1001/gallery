A Garden in Full Bloom

A painted memory of home · Oil on canvas by Li Chen Ma

"I planted every flower in that garden myself. Years of tending, pruning, and watching it grow — then one morning I looked out and thought: this is the most beautiful thing I have ever made. I had to paint it."

— Li Chen Ma

When Li Chen Ma and her family moved into their new home, the garden was bare. She had a vision — and over the years that followed, she brought it to life: bougainvillea in full bloom, two deck chairs set up on the limestone terrace -- a spot we love to watch the mountian sunsets from. Succulents planted on the gravel add color to the garden, making it look even more lush. It became not just a garden but a living work of art, tended daily with the same patience and care she brought to her canvases.

A garden takes years to become itself. By the time mine was truly beautiful, I had already fallen in love with it — the way you love something that grew slowly, through your own hands.

She painted it at its peak — a summer morning when every bloom was open at once and the light fell through the leaves in long, warm shafts. Working from photographs she had taken over many seasons, she composed the painting to capture not just a single moment but the accumulated feeling of the garden at its very best: the layered depth of greens, the warmth of sunlight on petals, the almost overwhelming abundance of color.

The house and garden are no longer hers. But this painting is. Every brushstroke holds a memory: a specific morning, a particular fragrance, the quiet satisfaction of having grown something beautiful from nothing. It is the kind of painting that brings warmth to a room not just through its colors, but through the feeling it carries — of home, of care, of seasons well spent.

Hung in a living room or study, especially through the cooler months, it does what the best interiors art does: it makes a space feel inhabited by a life fully lived.
